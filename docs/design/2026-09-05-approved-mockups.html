Plate 02

Join or open a table

Two ways in, side by side. Joining is the common case, so it sits first and the code field is the largest thing on the screen.

  • Codes avoid O, 0, I, 1, S, 5 — nobody should mistype a code read aloud over voice chat.
  • Buy-in is set when you open the table, not after people arrive.

Plate 03

Waiting on the table

The code is the biggest thing here, because for thirty seconds it is the only thing anyone needs. Rules sit beside the seats so latecomers can read what they are joining.

  • Every optional rule is a toggle the host controls. Guests see them read-only.
  • Bots are only offered when the buy-in is zero — they have no chips to lose.

Plate 04 · live

The table

The screen that matters. This one works — click dice to set them aside and watch the score and the odds move. It runs the real scoring rules.

  • Dice that cannot score are visibly dead and refuse the click.
  • The bust chance is shown, not hidden. The game's whole tension is that number against your turn total.
  • Bank is disabled until the selection is legal — you cannot bank a mistake.

What I need from you

The direction

Is this the back room you pictured? If it reads too austere or too antique, say which — the palette and the type are the two dials.

The table

Plate 04 is the screen players stare at for an hour. Play with the dice. Does setting them aside feel right, and is the bust number in the right place?

What's missing

Chat has no screen here yet, and neither does the solo bot practice game. Tell me if either needs to be visible rather than tucked away.

One deliberate omission

These stay dark whatever your system theme. The game is a dim room. If you want a light mode, that is a real decision to make now, not later.
